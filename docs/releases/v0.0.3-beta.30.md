# Keybumps 0.0.3-beta.30

Quick Search is now the window's name, and Settings › Quick Search lets you reorder its tabs, hide the ones you don't use, and keep ⌘1 to ⌘9 on the tabs you do.

## Quick Search

- The window that ⌘Space opens is called Quick Search everywhere in the app, where some text still said Command Palette. Its first tab, for apps, files, folders, snippets, emoji, and commands, is the **Search tab**.
- Quick Search opens on the first tab shown, and ←/→ move through the tabs in your order.
- A tab's ⌘ number is its place among the tabs shown: ⌘1 is the first, ⌘2 the second. Hiding a tab moves the keys of the tabs after it up.

## Settings › Quick Search

- The page lists every tab with its key. Drag a tab to reorder, as in any Mac list, or use ▲▼. **Reset Order** puts the tabs back as they were and leaves which tabs show alone.
- Each tab has a switch to show or hide it, so you can hide the Search tab too. One tab always stays shown. A tab whose plugin is off says so under its name and has no key.
- Quick Search no longer has a switch to turn it off, in Settings › Plugins or on its own page. Hide its Search tab instead. If you'd had Quick Search off, the Search tab starts hidden, and Keybumps turns off Spotlight's ⌘Space shortcut once.
- The Hotkeys tab's switch moved here from Settings › Shortcut Coach. The tab still starts hidden.
