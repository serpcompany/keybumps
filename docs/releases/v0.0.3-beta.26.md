# Keybumps 0.0.3-beta.26

Screenshot Tools is now ScreenGrabs, and it records your screen too: trim, crop, and blur a recording before you save it. Keystrokes, a new plugin, shows the shortcuts you press.

## ScreenGrabs

- Screenshot Tools is now called **ScreenGrabs**, and so is its ⌘3 tab. Your screenshot shortcuts and settings are as you left them.
- The ⌘3 tab keeps your 50 newest screenshots however much you copy. Clipboard History now keeps them apart from your 50 newest other items, so a screenshot never pushes out a copy either.
- On macOS 14, ScreenGrabs takes screenshots as before, says recording needs macOS 15, and leaves ⇧⌘5 to macOS.

## New: Record your screen

On macOS 15 or later, ScreenGrabs records an area, a window, a screen, or all your screens, with your voice and the Mac's sound. Recording needs the same Screen Recording access as screenshots. Microphone access records your voice, and Input Monitoring shows the shortcuts you press; both are optional.

- Press ⇧⌘5, or type "record" in Quick Search, for **Record Screen**. ⇧⌘5 now opens it in place of macOS's screenshot toolbar, as Keybumps does ⇧⌘3 and ⇧⌘4. To keep macOS's, give Record Screen another shortcut in Settings › ScreenGrabs.
- The picker starts on Screen, then opens the way you last recorded. A window recording shows only that window, without its menus or popovers; record an area or the screen to include them.
- While you record, a small bar shows the time and lets you pause, draw, mute the microphone or the Mac's sound, restart, discard, or stop. It's never in the video. The time also shows in the menu bar, with Stop and Pause in its menu.
- Draw on the screen as you record with a pen, an arrow, a highlighter, or a rectangle. The shortcuts you press show in the video, never what you type. Turn on **Highlight clicks** to show each click.
- Esc never ends a recording or its countdown. Use the countdown's **Cancel** or the bar's **Discard**.

## Edit a recording

- When you stop, the recording opens in a big window to play and scrub, with **Discard**, **Copy** (⌘C), and **Save** (↩) in the same bar as the Screenshot Editor. Closing the window saves too.
- **Trim** the ends, or select a part and choose **Cut Section**. Trimmed and cut parts stay on the timeline, dimmed, so you can bring them back.
- **Crop** freely or to 16:9 or 1:1, and **Blur** what shouldn't show, for as long as you choose on the timeline.
- With edits, Save and Copy write the edited recording in place of the original, and closing the window asks "Save your edits?".
- Recordings are kept in Documents › Keybumps › captures. **Show in Finder** in Settings › ScreenGrabs opens the folder.

## New: Keystrokes

Keystrokes is a new plugin that shows the shortcuts you press on screen, for demos, recordings, and screen sharing. It's off until you turn it on in Settings › Plugins, and it needs Input Monitoring access.

- It shows only shortcuts, such as ⌘C, unless you choose All keys in Settings › Keystrokes. Nothing shows while you type a password.
- Choose Keycaps or the classic Bezel style, where the keys show, their size, and how long they stay. **Name the action** adds "Copy" beside ⌘C, and **Show clicks too** shows each click.
- **Show Keystrokes** in the Keybumps menu, or Show Keystrokes and Hide Keystrokes in Quick Search, take the keys off the screen or bring them back without turning the plugin off. You can also give Show & Hide Keystrokes a shortcut.
- macOS's and Keybumps' screenshot shortcuts never show, and pressing one clears the keys from the screen.

## Dictation

- **Automatic** is the new default Recognition language in Settings › Dictation. With a model that knows more than one language, Dictation recognizes the one you speak in each recording, and Dictation History shows it. With any other model, Settings says which language Automatic uses.
- If you never chose a language, Dictation now uses Automatic instead of English. A language you chose is kept.
