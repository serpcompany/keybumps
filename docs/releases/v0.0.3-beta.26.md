# Keybumps 0.0.3-beta.26

Record your screen with your voice, draw on it as you go, and show the shortcuts you press.

## New: Screencast

Screencast is a new plugin for macOS 15 or later. It's off until you turn it on in Settings › Plugins, and it needs Screen Recording access.

- Pick **Start Screencast** in Quick Search, or give it a shortcut in Settings › Screencast.
- Drag an area, click a window, or choose a screen or All Screens. Then take a screenshot at once, or record a video after a short countdown.
- Videos record your voice and the Mac's sound. Switch either off in the picker, or for every recording in Settings › Screencast.
- While you record, a small bar shows the time and lets you pause, draw, mute the microphone or the Mac's sound, restart, discard, or stop. It's never in the video. Restart and Discard ask first.
- The time also shows beside the Keybumps icon in the menu bar, with Stop and Pause in its menu.
- Draw with a pen, arrow, highlighter, or rectangle. Marks fade after a few seconds, or stay until you clear them. Escape stops drawing, Delete clears, and ⌘Z undoes the last mark.
- The shortcuts you press show along the bottom of what you record. What you type never does. Turn on **Highlight clicks** to show each click in the video.
- A window recording shows just that window, without its menus or popovers. To include them, record an area or the screen.

## After each capture

- A panel opens in the corner with the capture, a one-line note, the type (Bug, Feature, or Feedback), and a GitHub repository, which Keybumps guesses from the app or website you were in. It remembers your corrections.
- Trim a video, or open a screenshot in the Screenshot Editor. A screenshot can also go to the Screenshots tab (⌘3).
- **Save** keeps the capture with its note. **Copy** also puts it on the clipboard, without adding it to Clipboard History. **Discard** asks, then deletes it. Return saves, and so does closing the panel.
- Captures are kept in Documents › Keybumps › captures. **Show in Finder** in Settings › Screencast opens the folder.

## New: Keystrokes

Keystrokes is a new plugin that shows the shortcuts you press on screen, for demos, recordings, and screen sharing. It's off until you turn it on in Settings › Plugins, and it needs Input Monitoring access.

- By default it shows only shortcuts, such as ⌘C, not what you type. Choose All keys in Settings › Keystrokes to show your typing too. Nothing shows while you type a password.
- Choose Keycaps or the classic Bezel style, and where the keys show, their size, and how long they stay. **Name the action** adds "Copy" beside ⌘C, and **Show clicks too** shows each click.
- **Show Keystrokes** in the Keybumps menu hides or shows the keys without turning the plugin off. You can also give Show & Hide Keystrokes a shortcut.
- macOS's and Keybumps's screenshot shortcuts never show, and pressing one clears the keys from the screen.

## Quick Search

- Type "screencast" for **Start Screencast**, or "keystrokes" for **Show Keystrokes** or **Hide Keystrokes**. Each is listed while its plugin is on.
